When a burst pipe is an emergency

A split pipe is pouring water into your walls, floor or ceiling, and every minute adds damage. Our line is answered by a person 24/7 in Etna, and the first thing we will ask is whether the main valve is closed. Close it first, then call.

A burst pipe is a pipe that has split, cracked or separated at a joint and now lets water escape faster than a drip. The usual causes are freezing, age and corrosion, and over-tightened or failed fittings.

Once the water is off, open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the lines and relieve pressure. If a ceiling is bulging with water, a small hole in the lowest point lets it drain into a bucket instead of collapsing.

Main valve first

It is usually in the basement, utility room or near the water heater, and turns clockwise to close. If it will not turn, the curb or meter shutoff is the next option.

Power and heater

Switch off the water heater at its breaker or gas control if the tank may drain. Do not touch outlets or switches in a flooded room.

Save what you can

Lift furniture and electronics out of the water path. Take photos before you move or mop anything.

Stopping the water: the order that limits damage

  1. 1

    Close the main shutoff

    Turn it fully clockwise. A gate valve takes several turns, and a lever-style ball valve takes a quarter turn so the handle sits across the pipe.

  2. 2

    Open the lowest tap

    This drains whatever is still in the pipes. Then open an upstairs tap to let air in so the lines empty faster.

  3. 3

    Turn off the water heater

    An empty line can let an electric heater run dry and burn out its elements. Cut the breaker or set the gas control to pilot.

  4. 4

    Contain the water

    Buckets under drips, towels at doorways, and a wet vacuum for standing water. Water running down a wall can follow wiring, so keep the room's lights off if they are wet.

  5. 5

    Check the pipe from a distance

    Look for the split but do not touch a bare pipe that is near wiring. Note where it is for the plumber.

  6. 6

    Call and describe it

    Tell us the pipe material if you can see it, how big the wet area is, and whether the water is still running.

Repair methods by pipe material

The right fix depends on what the pipe is made of and how much of the line has weakened.

Pipe materialTypical failureCommon repair
CopperFreeze splits, pinholes, failed solder jointsCut out the damaged section, then sweat or press-fit a new piece with couplings
PEXFrozen and swollen sections, cracked fittingsReplace the section with crimp or expansion fittings, since PEX often tolerates a freeze better than rigid pipe
Galvanized steelCorrosion thinning, split threaded jointsSplice in new pipe with unions, or plan a larger replacement if the rest is thin
CPVCBrittle cracks, failed glued jointsCut and re-solvent weld with new pipe and couplings
Drain pipe (PVC, cast iron)Cracked fittings, broken hubsReplace the cracked piece with a repair coupling or new section

When the metal is old everywhere, a fix at one spot can be followed by a new failure beside it. We tell you when that is likely.

Frozen pipes: why they split and how to thaw one safely

Water expands as it freezes, and the pressure builds in the stretch of pipe between the ice and a closed faucet. The split often appears in the part that is not frozen yet, and it only starts to leak when the ice melts. That is why a pipe can look fine at 5 a.m. and flood at noon.

If a faucet runs only a trickle in cold weather, assume a frozen pipe. Open the tap and warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or heating pad, starting near the faucet and working back toward the frozen part. Never use an open flame, which can ignite framing and damage the pipe. If you can see a split, close the main and call, because it will leak once it thaws.

Temporary clamp or proper repair

A repair clamp can buy time, but it is not the same as replacing the damaged piece.

Temporary clamp or wrap

  • Slows or stops a small leak long enough to get through a night
  • Works best on a straight, clean section of pipe
  • Fails on split fittings, corroded steel and pipe that keeps moving
  • Leaves a weak spot hidden behind the wrap

Proper repair

  • Cuts out the damaged section and installs new pipe with proper fittings
  • Includes checking the rest of the line near the break for further damage
  • Restores the pipe to normal pressure and temperature use
  • Lets you close up the wall or ceiling with confidence in what is behind it

After the pipe is fixed: drying out and finding the damage path

Water travels. It runs down studs, soaks insulation, and settles under floors well away from the break. We trace the damage path with a moisture meter so you know which walls and floors need to dry, and which are fine.

Drywall and insulation that stayed wet for more than a day or two often need to come out. Carpet padding is rarely worth saving after a soak. Drying and rebuilding belong to restoration contractors and carpenters, not us, but we make sure the plumbing is sound before anything is closed up. If the cause was a slow seep and not a sudden split, leak detection finds the source.

Cold snaps and frozen pipes in Etna Township

The published cold normals for central Ohio come from the Columbus airport, and they show how often a pipe here is put under stress.

Nights that matter

January's average low is about 22 F, and a typical year brings about 34 days at or below 20 F and about 103 days at or below 32 F. Each of those nights is a chance for a pipe in an exterior wall, garage or crawlspace to freeze, so keep a faucet dripping on the coldest wall from December on.

Extreme cold on record

NOAA logged wind chills of 20 to 40 below zero on 30 January 2019 and below -25 F on 22 December 2022. Wind chill does not freeze water inside a heated wall, but it pushes cold air through gaps into attics, rim joists and crawlspaces, which is where pipes split.

Mixed house ages

Most township homes were built from 1990 to 2009, with a smaller group from the 1970s and a few farmhouses older than that near the village. A 1970s house may still carry galvanized or early copper lines, so when one pipe splits there, look at the lines beside it before closing the wall.

Plumbers must be registered, and a permit is required to repair plumbing apart from swapping a fixture for a similar one in the same spot. Ask us whether your repair needs one.
