Sewer line failing: what to do while you wait

Sewer line repair in Etna means restoring a failed length of the buried pipe between your house and the public sewer or septic tank. If sewage is coming up in the house or a sinkhole has opened in the yard, a person answers our line 24/7. The steps below keep the damage from spreading.

A broken sewer line can look like a clog, but cabling a collapsed pipe only pushes soil further in. Stop water use and let us find out which problem you have.

Stop using water

Every gallon going down a broken pipe soaks the soil around it.

Mark wet or sunken ground

Take a photo of the yard and note where it is soft.

Keep kids and pets away

Sewage-soaked soil and an open cleanout are both unsafe.

Do not dig

Pipes, gas and electric can share the same ground.

Repair methods and their trade-offs

The right method depends on what the camera shows. Here is how the main options compare.

MethodBest forTrade-off
Spot repair (dig and replace a section)One cracked or offset joint, with the rest of the pipe soundNeeds a trench at the break, so yard or driveway disturbance is limited to that spot.
Pipe lining (cured-in-place)Long runs of cracked or root-prone pipe that has not collapsedLess digging, but the pipe must be cleaned first and a collapsed pipe cannot be lined.
Pipe burstingPipe too far gone to line, but a path clear enough to pull new pipe throughEntry and exit pits are needed, and nearby utilities limit where it is possible.
Full open-trench replacementCollapsed, badly bellied or mixed-material linesMost disruption, but it lets us reset the slope and replace every bad section.

No method suits every line. We explain which ones apply once we have seen the footage.

Camera first, repair second

Every sewer repair starts with a camera, because the plan depends on where the damage is, how long it runs and what the pipe is made of. We feed the camera from the cleanout, record the run, and mark the location of any defect with a locator at the surface.

That tells us whether we are looking at a single bad joint, a stretch of root-invaded clay, or a line that has lost its slope for twenty feet. You see the same footage we do. For stand-alone inspections, including house purchases, see sewer inspection.

Signs the sewer line itself is damaged

A clog is a blockage. A damaged line has symptoms that return after cleaning.

Repeated backups after cleaning

The same pipe keeps catching waste because of roots, a sag or a broken joint.

Sunken or soggy patch in the yard

Soil is washing into or out of the pipe along the line.

Sewer smell outdoors or in the basement

A crack, an open joint or a dry trap lets gas escape.

Slow drains throughout the house

Several fixtures slow together points to the main, and a cleaning that fades quickly points to the pipe itself.

Lush green strip in the lawn

Leaking sewage fertilizes the grass above it.

What the pipe is made of matters

The age of the house is a rough guide to the pipe material, and each material fails in its own way.

Cast iron

Common in older homes. It corrodes from the inside and scales, and joints can crack or separate.

Clay tile

Older still. Short sections with many joints invite roots, and tile cracks under load or soil shift.

Orangeburg (bituminized fiber)

Used in some mid-century houses. It softens, flattens and delaminates, so it is rarely a candidate for lining.

PVC and ABS

Newer plastic lines resist roots and corrosion, but can sag if the trench was poorly bedded or crack from impact.

Where a repair stops on a septic property

The pipe from the house to the tank is plumbing we can repair. The tank, the distribution box and the drain field are septic work, and we do not pump or repair those.

If the camera shows the line is sound and the problem lies at the tank, we say so and point you to a septic service rather than dig up a healthy pipe.

Planning a sewer repair in Etna Township

A few local details shape the plan, whether the job is in Etna or a neighbor such as Pataskala, Kirkersville or Reynoldsburg.

Call before any dig

Ohio 811 (OHIO811) needs at least two working days of notice before digging, so utility marks are on the ground before a trench opens. That timing belongs in your repair schedule.

Your lot, not the soil map

The mapped soil at our own address is a moderately well drained glacial till (Cardington silt loam). That is one data point, so the locator and a test hole decide depth and trench plan on your lot.

Who owns which pipe

Storm sewers in the public right-of-way are the township's, while private pipes and ditches are the owner's. If your street is sewered at all, ask the district where its sewer ends and yours begins.

A mixed housing stock

Most township homes date from 1990 to 2009, so a plastic line with a bad joint or a sag is common. A few farmhouses older than 1940 may hold clay or iron. If it only clogs, see sewer line cleaning.
