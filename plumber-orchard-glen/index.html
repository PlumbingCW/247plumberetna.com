Where Orchard Glen sits

Orchard Glen is a residential subdivision in Etna Township, Licking County. The township long-range plan places Etna Cemetery between Orchard Glen and the Peppertree subdivision, on the south side of Pike Street. Census address matching puts Orchard Glen Drive and Winesap Street inside the township and inside the Etna census designated place.

Those addresses carry ZIP 43062, a code the city of Pataskala shares. A 43062 mailing address therefore does not tell you which government covers your lot. Orchard Glen sits in the township, so plumbing permits and plumber registration run through the Licking County Health Department. The Pataskala page fits only homes inside that city.

Which first call fits what you notice

Most calls from a subdivision like this sort into five patterns. The first column is the quickest way to find out who should hear about it first.

What you noticeWho handles it firstWhere to read more
Water bubbling up in the street or yard before the meterThe Licking Regional Water District, which owns the line to the meterWater service line repair
Wet spot or low pressure with the meter reading still movingYour side of the meter, which is a plumber's jobLeak detection
Several drains gurgle or back up at onceThe main line from the house, so a plumber firstSewer line cleaning
One slow sink or tubA local clog in a single branchDrain cleaning
No hot water or a wet floor near the tankThe water heater itselfWater heater repair

If you cannot tell whether the problem is before or after the meter, call us anyway. We help you work out which side it is on.

Who owns which pipe

The Licking Regional Water District supplies public water across much of Etna Township and runs sewer service in parts of it. Its office is at 8675 York Road and its number is 740-927-0410. The district has not published a list of which streets it sewers, so ask it about your address.

As a general rule, the utility handles its main and the owner handles the line from there into the house, but each utility sets its own boundary. Ask the district exactly where your responsibility starts before you pay for a repair. We then fix everything on your side, from the camera inspection to the repair itself.

Before you call about a backup

These take five minutes.

  1. 1

    Find your cleanout

    Look for a capped pipe near the foundation, in the basement or in the garage. Do not open it if sewage is already pushing up.

  2. 2

    Stop adding water

    Hold off on laundry and dishwashers. Every gallon has to go somewhere.

  3. 3

    Note which fixtures are affected

    A single slow sink points to a branch. Several backing up together point to the main line.

  4. 4

    Tell us the street

    Give the street name when you call and say whether you know if you are on public sewer or a septic tank.

What public records do not tell us

We found no public record describing the pipe material, sewer layout or build dates of Orchard Glen homes. We therefore do not guess at them, and you should be wary of anyone who does.

A sewer camera inspection shows what your own line is made of and what shape it is in. That one picture is more useful than any neighborhood average.
