Small commercial, said plainly

We work on shops, offices, small restaurants and light retail buildings. We do not take on industrial process piping, high-rise risers or large mechanical plants. If a job is outside what we do, we say so on the first call and send you toward someone who can help.

After-hours leak or backup in your building

When water is on a floor in a business, the clock runs against your stock, your tenants and your floor. A person answers our line 24/7, and the first useful step is to close the main shutoff, usually near the meter or where the supply enters the building. If sewage is rising from a floor drain, stop using restrooms and sinks until it is cleared.

Keep customers and staff away from standing water near panels, registers or coolers. Move stock, photograph the damage and note the time. We schedule the next available appointment and tell you what we will need when we arrive.

Close the main

Find it before you need it. Tag it so a night manager can locate it without calling anyone.

Stop the drains

Sinks, dishwashers and restrooms all feed the same line. One flush can send more water onto the floor.

Open the building

Have the building opened, the mechanical room reachable and the alarm code ready for whoever arrives.

Commercial leak detection, kept honest

Finding a leak in a commercial building starts with the meter. Close every fixture, read the meter, wait, then read it again, and any movement means water is escaping somewhere. We then use pressure tests, acoustic listening and a moisture meter to narrow the area.

Not every leak call ends with a repair on the same day. Large slabs, shared walls and buried lines take longer to confirm, and some need other trades to open finishes. Our leak detection page explains the methods in more detail.

How a commercial drain call usually goes

Drain calls make up most of what small businesses send us. The order of operations is deliberate, because a blocked floor drain and a blocked main look alike at first.

  1. 1

    Locate the stoppage

    We check which fixtures are slow. One fixture means a branch line, several mean the main, and a floor drain with sewage suggests the main.

  2. 2

    Cable or jet

    A cable opens a clean path through soft blockages. Hard scale, grease and roots call for hydro jetting.

  3. 3

    Camera the line

    A camera shows broken pipe, offset joints and root intrusion, and confirms the line is clear afterward.

  4. 4

    Report back

    You get the explanation in plain terms, with the next steps if the line needs repair.

Commercial water heaters and backflow assemblies

Two items that sit in the background of most business buildings, and both are easy to ignore until something fails.

Water heaters

  • Commercial buildings may run a tank, a tankless unit or a heater shared by tenants
  • Sizing follows peak use, not square footage
  • Sediment shortens tank life when it is not flushed
  • A leaking tank is replaced rather than patched

Backflow assemblies

  • A backflow assembly protects the public water supply from your building
  • Many systems require periodic testing, and who must test is set locally
  • The water district can tell you which rules cover your building
  • We can discuss repair of an assembly when it is found to leak

Restrooms, the busiest plumbing in any building

Public restrooms run through hundreds of flushes a week, so flappers, fill valves and flush valves wear out faster than in a house. Flushometer toilets, wall-hung sinks and floor-mounted fixtures each need their own parts, and ADA-height fixtures need the right clearances.

A restroom that is out of order costs a business more than the repair does, so we aim to carry common parts and tell you honestly when something must be ordered. A running flush valve or a slow drain is cheaper to fix on a planned visit than at midnight.

Who licenses and inspects business plumbing here

A shop or office in Etna Township, or in Reynoldsburg, which borders the township to the west, deals with more than one office. Knowing which does what saves a stalled project, and we sort it out on the first call.

State licensing

The Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board licenses contractors for commercial work. Ask any contractor to show you the license, ours included, before work starts.

County registration

Plumbers working in Licking County must be registered with the county health department, and a permit is required for new commercial plumbing.

Backflow program

The Licking Regional Water District runs a backflow prevention program. Ask the district what applies to your building before you schedule testing or repair.

Your side of the line

The township maintains storm sewers in the public right-of-way. Pipes and ditches on your own property are the owner's job. Also see Pataskala and Kirkersville for neighboring service.
