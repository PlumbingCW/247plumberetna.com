A fast-growing city, and what that does to the plumbing

Pataskala had about 18,500 residents at the Census Bureau's 2025 estimate, up from 17,886 at the 2020 count. Census housing figures show roughly 48 percent of its 7,165 homes were built in 2000 or later, and about 69 percent are detached single-family houses. The newer share is large.

That shapes the calls we get. A house from the 2000s is often on its second water heater, with fixtures and shutoff valves that are wearing out together. Appliances and softeners that were fine at move-in start leaking at the same time, which is why a water heater replacement call here often turns into a list of other small jobs.

Three layers of water and sewer in one city

A Pataskala address can be on the City of Pataskala utility, on the Licking Regional Water District, or on a private well and septic system. The city publishes an after-hours emergency line for its utility at 740-927-6867. The district publishes 740-927-0410 and asks callers to press 5 for a water emergency or 6 for a sanitary sewer emergency.

Which one serves your street is something we cannot guess from the city name alone. If water is bubbling up in the street or yard before the meter, that is the utility's line to call. Inside the house, it is ours. Our water service line page explains how to tell the two apart.

Pipe split or sewer backing up in Pataskala? The first ten minutes

A person answers our emergency line 24/7, and you can start the steps below before the call ends. They apply on city water, on the district or on a well.

Water that is still moving does the most damage, so stopping it comes before everything else.

Close the main valve

Turn the shutoff clockwise where the line enters the house. On a well, switch off the pump breaker so the pressure tank stops feeding the leak.

Stop the drains

If the lowest fixture is backing up, no laundry, no flushing and no dishwasher until it is cleared.

Kill the water heater

A leaking tank needs its power or gas off and the cold valve on top closed.

Note where the water is

Inside the house, call us. Outside before the meter, the utility should hear about it too.

The river, Mile Run and wet basements

The South Fork Licking River and Mile Run cross the city, and FEMA flood maps put roughly 3 percent of the city, about 0.9 square miles, in mapped special flood hazard zones. A federal disaster declaration for severe storms and flooding in August 2026 also covers Licking County, though no damage was tied to Pataskala in what we reviewed.

Most basements are nowhere near those zones, but groundwater still rises after hard rain. Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the float lift before the next storm. If it hesitates, our sump pump page covers repair and backups, and basement waterproofing covers the drainage side.

Permits: the county health department, with a branch in city hall

Plumbing permits in Licking County come from the Licking County Health Department, and plumbers must be registered with it. The department has a Pataskala branch office inside city hall at 621 West Broad Street. The city's own permit page lists zoning permits and no plumbing trade permits.

That county wording is not specific to Pataskala, so we confirm with the health department by phone when a job is borderline. The county rule excepts like-for-like fixture swaps in the same location. We tell you what applies at your address before work starts.

The historic core around Main and High Streets

Pataskala was laid out in 1851 when the railroad arrived, and nine properties in the city are on the National Register of Historic Places. The oldest housing is generally near the original center, so a camera look at the drain line is usually the right first step.

Broad Street and the State Route 310 corridor

SR 16 and SR 310 are the two state routes through the city. Homes and small businesses along them often have a mix of original and replaced plumbing.

Newer subdivisions such as The Settlement and Brooksedge

City records name both. Houses built since 2000 mean water heaters, softeners and fixture valves are the common calls.

Corporate Park and the commercial edge

The city's corporate park covers nearly a thousand acres between Refugee Road and Broad Street. Business plumbing is covered on our commercial plumbing page.

New water and sewer lines are going in

The Licking Regional Water District announced a Summit Road to Morse Road extension in February 2026, with about 11,400 feet of waterline and nearly 10,300 feet of sanitary sewer. The district's notice says residents are not required to connect, which means some properties nearby remain on private systems.

If your street gets a line, the plumbing on your side is yours: a service line from the meter to the house and, for a sewer tie-in, the house line itself. A sewer inspection before you connect shows whether the house line is sound.
