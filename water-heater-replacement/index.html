Tank leaking or no hot water? Do this first

A water heater that is leaking or has stopped heating is an urgent problem, and a person answers our line around the clock. While you wait, switch off the breaker for an electric unit or turn a gas unit's control to off, then close the cold supply valve on the pipe feeding the top of the tank.

Water heater replacement is the removal of a failed tank-style or tankless unit and the installation of a new one, including the supply lines, gas or electrical connection, venting and safety valves. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, including after hours for a tank that is actively leaking.

Cut the power

At the panel, switch off the double-pole breaker marked for the water heater. Standing water and a live 240-volt element do not mix.

Close the cold valve

The shutoff handle sits on the cold pipe above the tank. Turn it until it stops and the flow into the tank halts.

Protect the floor

Set a bucket under any drip, move boxes and laundry out of the puddle, and keep pets and children away from the area.

Photograph the rating plate

The label lists capacity, fuel type and model. A clear photo helps us plan the right replacement before we arrive.

Why tanks leak, and what that means for your decision

Most tanks fail from the inside out. The steel shell sits in water all day, and the sacrificial anode rod that protects it is slowly used up. After that, corrosion works on the liner and the welded seams. A weep at the bottom seam or a steady trickle from under the jacket usually means the tank itself has failed, and no fitting or valve will change that.

Not every puddle means a dead tank. Water can come from a loose supply connection, a dripping drain valve, or the discharge pipe of the relief valve. We trace the source first, because a short valve fix on a sound tank is a different job from a replacement. The full list of leak sources is on our water heater repair page.

If the leak is coming from the tank, the unit has to go. A failed tank cannot be patched, and running it until it floods risks the drywall, the flooring and anything stored nearby. Basement and garage installs often sit near a floor drain, and a drain pan piped to that drain is what limits the damage the next time something fails.

How a replacement goes once we are at the house

  1. 1

    Confirm the cause

    We test the tank, the connections and the relief valve so a replacement happens only when the old unit is really finished.

  2. 2

    Shut down and drain

    Power or gas goes off, the cold supply is closed, and the tank drains to a floor drain or through a hose to the outdoors. A full 50-gallon tank takes a while to empty, and we plan for that.

  3. 3

    Remove the old unit

    The flue, supply lines and electrical connection come apart and the old tank is carried out. Old tank removed from the property is part of the job.

  4. 4

    Set, connect and vent

    The new heater goes on a drain pan with new flexible connectors, a fresh relief valve and discharge pipe, and a flue or vent run checked for slope and clearance.

  5. 5

    Fill, purge and test

    The tank fills with a hot tap open so air can escape. Then power or gas comes back on, we confirm the flame or elements are working, and we check every joint for weeping.

Choosing the new unit: size, fuel and venting

The replacement does not have to match the old tank, and sometimes it should not.

Capacity

A 40-gallon tank suits many households of two or three people, and a 50-gallon tank is common for four or more. The first-hour rating on the yellow EnergyGuide label says more than gallons alone, since it shows how much hot water the unit delivers in a busy hour.

Fuel type

Gas for gas, or electric for electric, keeps the job simple. A fuel change is possible but adds a gas line, a new 240-volt circuit, or both, so it is a bigger project than a same-day swap.

Venting

Atmospheric gas units draw air from the room and vent up a flue. Power-vent and direct-vent models use a fan and plastic pipe through a wall, which lets a heater move away from an old chimney.

Expansion and pressure

When a check valve or pressure reducing valve closes the plumbing system, heated water has nowhere to expand. A small expansion tank on the cold line absorbs that growth and spares the relief valve.

No hot water, but nothing is leaking

Sometimes the tank is dry and sound but the water is cold. On an electric unit that usually points to a tripped breaker, a burned-out element or a failed thermostat. On a gas unit it is more often a pilot that will not stay lit or a worn thermocouple.

Those are frequently repairs, so we check them first. Replacement comes into play when the tank is old, rusted at the base, or has already been repaired more than once. If your heater is part of a bigger remodel, planned water heater installation gives more room to choose the size and location.
