What boiler repair covers here

Boiler repair is the diagnosis and fixing of the water, pressure and piping side of a heating boiler: leaks, valves, circulator pumps, expansion tanks, air in the lines, and the pipework to radiators or baseboards. We work on hot water boilers and on steam systems. Where a fault sits in the burner controls or a sealed manufacturer part, we tell you rather than guess.

Boilers heat a house by moving hot water or steam through a closed loop, which is why a small leak or a bad valve can cost you the whole system. If the issue is hot water for taps rather than heat, water heater repair is the better page.

If you smell gas, leave first

A rotten-egg smell or a carbon monoxide alarm means leave the building, then call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not turn lights or switches on or off, and do not call a plumber before you are out. Once the utility clears the building, we can look at what failed.

Four boiler problems we see most

Most service calls fall into one of four groups. Naming yours helps us bring the right parts.

No heat

The boiler runs but rooms stay cold, or it does not fire at all. Causes include a failed circulator, a stuck zone valve, air trapped in the loop, or a safety lockout.

Leaks

Drips at a relief valve, a pump flange, a radiator valve or a pipe joint. A leak that drains pressure usually calls for a quick look because the system can shut down on low water.

Pressure swings

Pressure that keeps climbing or dropping points at an expansion tank, an auto-fill valve or a leaking relief valve.

Noise

Banging, gurgling or kettle sounds suggest trapped air, scale, low water or water hammer in steam piping.

Hot water boilers and steam boilers

They look similar from the basement but behave very differently, and so do their problems.

FeatureHot water (hydronic)Steam
What carries heatHeated water pumped through baseboards or radiatorsSteam rises to radiators and condenses back to water
Normal pressureOften around 12 to 15 psi when coldWell under that, usually just a few psi
Key partsCirculator, expansion tank, zone valves, air ventsSight glass, low-water cutoff, radiator vents, return lines
Common faultsAir in the loop, failed pump, pressure swingsLow water, clogged vents, banging pipes

Check your boiler's own gauge and manual. Normal readings vary with the system.

How we work through a no-heat call

  1. 1

    Check the basics

    We confirm thermostat calls, power to the boiler, water level or pressure, and whether the unit is locked out.

  2. 2

    Read the pressure and flow

    Gauges tell us if the system is low, high or air-bound. We bleed radiators or purge zones if air is the cause.

  3. 3

    Test pumps and valves

    We feel for flow, test the circulator and zone valves, and look at the expansion tank and relief valve.

  4. 4

    Repair or hand off

    Plumbing-side faults get repaired. If the cause is in the burner controls, the heat exchanger or a sealed part, we tell you and explain the next step.

What we handle, and what belongs elsewhere

A clear scope saves you a wasted visit.

Plumbing-side work we do

  • Leaking valves, pump flanges and pipe joints.
  • Circulator pump and zone valve replacement.
  • Expansion tank, fill valve and relief valve problems.
  • Bleeding air from radiators and purging zones.

When it is HVAC or manufacturer territory

  • Burner, ignition and combustion tuning.
  • Cracked heat exchangers or sealed control boards.
  • Warranty claims and factory-only parts.
  • Whole-system replacement planning, which we can discuss but may refer out.

Parts we replace most often

Circulator pump

The pump moves hot water through the loop. A noisy, hot or silent circulator often explains a cold house.

Expansion tank

A waterlogged tank lets pressure spike, which opens the relief valve and loses water.

Relief valve

A valve that has opened repeatedly may no longer seat. It is a safety device, never to be capped.

Air vents and zone valves

Vents release trapped air and zone valves direct heat room to room. Either one stuck can leave a zone cold.

Before you call: what to note

These details cut the diagnosis time.

  • ✓The type of boiler: hot water or steam, and the fuel it burns.
  • ✓What the pressure gauge reads and whether it has changed.
  • ✓Which rooms are cold and which are warm.
  • ✓Any error light or display message on the boiler.
  • ✓Whether water is dripping and from where.
