Main Sewer Line Repair Options in Etna, OH

Four ways to fix a damaged main line, and how a camera decides which one fits.

Spot repair to full replacementCamera firstOhio 811 and permits

By the Etna 24/7 Plumbing team

Speak with a plumber(888) 910-8644Call now

A main sewer line repair fixes the damaged section of pipe that carries wastewater from your house to the public sewer or the septic tank, and it is done in one of four ways: spot repair, pipe lining, pipe bursting or full replacement. The right choice depends on what is wrong, where it is and what sits above it.

No method is best everywhere. This post lays out how each one works, where each falls short and what to ask before you approve any of them. It assumes a cleaning has already shown that the pipe itself, not a simple clog, is the problem.

Start with a camera, not a guess

A sewer camera inspection shows the pipe's material, its joints, any cracks or offsets, the roots, and whether the line sags. A locator tool paired with the camera marks the depth and position of each problem on the surface, so a repair crew digs where the damage actually is.

The line usually has to be cleaned first, because grease and roots hide the wall. Sewer line cleaning is often the first visit for that reason. Cameras have limits of their own, since a total collapse blocks them and a pool in a sag can blur the picture.

Footage turns the decision into a matter of evidence. A single cracked joint calls for a different fix than a hundred feet of crushed pipe, and you can see the difference on the screen.

Material, depth, slope, nearby trees and your property layout all change which method is realistic.

Spot repair: replace only what failed

A spot repair makes sense when the camera finds one or two bad places and the rest of the pipe is sound. Crews dig down to the defect, cut out the damaged length and install new pipe with proper couplings.

The catch is the rest of the line. Old clay or fiber pipe often has more defects than the one you found, and a repair can leave you back in the same trench in a few years. Ask the plumber what the footage shows between the spots, not just at them.

Pipe lining: a new pipe inside the old one

Lining works through small access points, so it spares most of the yard. The old pipe is cleaned, a resin-saturated liner is pulled in, and it is cured to form a smooth continuous pipe. Cracks, leaking joints and root entry points get sealed in one pass.

It has real limits. The host pipe has to hold its shape, so a belly that holds water will still hold water after lining, and a crushed section needs digging first. A liner also slightly reduces the pipe's inside diameter, and connections at the house and the main need attention. A camera check afterward confirms the result.

Pipe bursting: pull the old pipe out of the way

Bursting replaces a failing line along its existing path with a minimum of digging. A bursting head is pulled through the old pipe, breaks it outward into the surrounding soil, and drags the new pipe in behind it.

It is not a fit for everything. Crews need entry and exit pits, other utilities close to the path have to be located and protected, and certain pipe materials or soil conditions make it a poor choice. A site visit with a locator shows whether the path is clear enough.

Full replacement: the open-trench answer

Open-trench replacement is the most disruptive method and, in some cases, the only sound one. A collapsed section, a severe sag or pipe that has separated cannot be lined or burst reliably. Digging lets the crew set new pipe at the right slope on a prepared bed.

Because every foot of the path is exposed, the crew can fix grade, replace fittings and reconnect the house and the main properly. Landscaping, concrete and fencing in the way need a plan for restoration, and you should ask what is included before work starts.

Mistakes that make homeowners pay twice

Most expensive second visits trace back to one of these.

Skipping the camera

Choosing a method without seeing the pipe is guessing, and the wrong guess can cost you the repair and a second visit.

Lining a sag

A liner follows the pipe it goes into. If the pipe holds water in a belly, the liner will too.

Patching one joint of many

A repaired joint next to five more weak joints just moves the next failure down the line.

Ignoring the tree

Roots that caused the damage will find the new joints too. Pipe type, joint style and tree location matter.

Forgetting the connections

The tie-in at the house and at the main is where many repairs leak. Ask how it will be done and checked.

Digging safely: Ohio 811 and permits

Anyone who digs in Ohio must notify the underground utility protection service, Ohio 811, at least two working days before excavating, not counting the day of the notice. Crews mark public lines with paint and flags so they do not hit gas, electric or telecom lines.

Utilities mark only their own lines. Private lines, such as an irrigation system, a well line or electric to an outbuilding, are usually not marked, so tell the crew what you know. Permit rules vary by job and jurisdiction, and we tell you what applies before work begins.

How Licking County regulates sewer plumbing work

In Licking County, plumbing is overseen by the Licking County Health Department. Plumbers must register with the department before working in the county, and a permit is required to install, extensively alter or repair plumbing, with an exception for swapping a fixture for the same or a similar one in the same location. For a sewer repair, expect the permit conversation to come up before anyone digs, and ask to see the permit number.

The county's regulation calls for rough-in, covered-work and final inspections. For a buried pipe, the covered-work inspection is the one that matters most, because it happens before the trench is backfilled. Ask your contractor when the inspector is coming so the pipe is still visible. The standards behind the inspections are the Ohio Plumbing Code, which is Chapter 4101:3 of the Ohio Administrative Code.

The regulation lets homeowners seek an exemption from registration for their own primary residence. Even so, a sewer line under a yard involves depth, shoring, grade and utility locating, and it is a poor first project. The department answers permit and registration questions by phone in the morning, and its Pataskala branch office is open for in-person business.

  • ✓What will the yard, driveway or landscaping look like afterward?
  • ✓How will the finished line be checked, and will I see a camera pass?
