Line packed solid and backing up right now

Hydro jetting is a drain cleaning method that sends high-pressure water through a hose and nozzle to scour grease, scale and roots off the inside of a pipe. If your line is overflowing, a person answers our line 24/7, and jetting may be the right tool once we see what is blocking it.

Jetting needs a clear path and a known pipe condition, so the first job is getting a picture of the situation. Use the minutes before we arrive to limit the water that reaches the blockage.

Shut off the washer and dishwasher

These push the most water, the fastest.

Do not flush

A toilet empties several gallons into a line that is already full.

Find your cleanout

A capped pipe in the basement, crawlspace or yard is where a jetter hose goes in. Clear the space around it.

Say what changed recently

Tree work, a long vacant stretch or a past root clog all steer the plan.

How a hydro jetter actually works

A trailer or truck-mounted unit pumps water from a tank through a flexible hose at pressures that run into the thousands of psi on larger machines, far beyond a garden hose. The hose ends in a nozzle with rear-facing jets. Those jets pull the hose forward along the pipe while they scour the wall and push debris back toward the opening.

That is the real difference from a cable. A cable spins a blade through the middle of the pipe and leaves the coating on the walls. Water moving at speed along every surface, around the full circumference, returns the pipe close to its original bore.

Where jetting beats cabling

Jetting earns its place on some blockages and is a waste of effort on others.

Grease and soap buildup

Kitchen and restaurant-style lines coat with fat that cables bore through. Water at pressure strips it from the pipe wall.

Scale in older iron

Cast iron pipes tuberculate over the decades. Jetting removes loose scale and widens the flow path without chewing the pipe.

Fine root hairs

A rotary nozzle clears hair-like roots at a joint. Thick woody roots may need a cutter head first, then a jetting pass.

Recurring clogs

If a main line has been cabled three times in a year, a full wall cleaning plus a camera check is usually the next step.

Before and after: how we confirm the result

We do not jet blind and we do not leave without checking the pipe afterward.

  1. 1

    Camera first

    A push camera goes through the cleanout so we can see the pipe material, any cracks, sags or offset joints, and what is actually blocking the flow.

  2. 2

    Choose the nozzle

    A penetrating nozzle drills through a hard plug, a rotary nozzle scours the wall, and a flat-spray tip is gentler for thin or old pipe.

  3. 3

    Jet in passes

    We advance the hose slowly, pull back along the wall, and repeat through problem sections. Pressure is set for the pipe, not the maximum the machine can deliver.

  4. 4

    Camera again

    The second pass shows whether the wall is clean. You see the same footage, and we explain what it shows in plain words.

When we will not jet a line

Water pressure that cleans a sound pipe can open up a weak one. If the camera shows a collapsed section, a crushed pipe, a large offset or an Orangeburg-type fiber pipe that has lost its shape, we stop and talk about repair instead. Jetting a pipe that is already failing can move the break and bring soil into the line.

In that case, the plumbing job becomes sewer line repair and the jetting waits until the pipe is sound enough to take it. We would rather tell you that in the first hour than clean a pipe that needs replacing.

Cable or jetter: a plain comparison

Cabling

  • Quick for a single sink or tub clog
  • Cuts through soft plugs and some roots
  • Leaves a film of grease on the pipe wall
  • Works where the line has bends a hose cannot take

Hydro jetting

  • Better for repeat clogs in kitchen and main lines
  • Cleans the entire circumference, not just a core
  • Needs an access point large enough for the hose
  • Needs a camera check first on old or damaged pipe

Jetting and what it does for the whole drain system

A jetted line stays open longer because the pipe wall is clean and the flow path is back to full size. That helps every fixture upstream, and it makes later camera work easier because the footage is not smeared with grease.

We pair jetting with simple habits at the sink and with a camera pass if the line was cabled repeatedly before. See drain cleaning for the everyday clog work, and sewer line cleaning when the blockage is at the main. If a camera finds roots in a joint, sewer inspection explains how we document the line.
