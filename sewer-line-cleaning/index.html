Sewer backup: what to do first

Sewer line cleaning means opening the main drain that carries every fixture's waste out of the house. When sewage comes up through a floor drain or tub, a person answers our line 24/7, and the steps below limit the damage before we get there.

A sewer backup is a health matter as well as a plumbing one, since the water carries bacteria. Keep children and pets out of the area and avoid touching standing water without waterproof gloves.

Stop all water use

That includes toilets, showers, washers and dishwashers.

Keep people out

Close off the room with the backup and do not wade through it barefoot.

Switch off power if water is near outlets

Only do this if you can reach the panel without standing in water.

Leave the cleanout capped

Do not open it yourself, since a full line can spill out under pressure.

Find your cleanout before we arrive

The cleanout is the capped pipe that gives a machine direct access to the main. Check these places.

  • ✓Basement floor, usually near where the main leaves the house
  • ✓Crawlspace wall or floor near the foundation
  • ✓Outside the foundation, a capped pipe in the ground near the side of the house
  • ✓Utility room or garage, sometimes a Y-fitting with a threaded plug
  • ✓Near the lowest bathroom if the house has a slab, since lines often exit near the bath group

Main line or a single branch

A main line problem and a branch problem look different if you watch which fixtures complain.

Multiple fixtures slow at once

Water from different branches meets at the main. A blockage there slows everything.

Floor drain or lowest tub backs up

These sit lower than other fixtures, so they overflow first.

Toilet gurgles when the washer drains

Displaced air is looking for a way out past a blockage.

Sewer smell in the basement

Gas rising from a dry trap or a partial blockage. A camera tells which.

Wet spot in the yard over the line

Sewage is escaping a crack or a bellied section and soaking the ground.

Cleaning the main sewer line

Main sewer cleaning uses a larger drum machine with a heavier cable than a sink machine, or a hydro jetter, fed through the cleanout. The cutter head is chosen for the pipe size and what we expect to meet, whether grease, paper or roots.

We work toward the blockage slowly, then clear it and flush the line with full water flow to confirm it drains freely. If the cable brings back root tissue, a heavy paper clump or a scale-laden cutter, the debris tells us why the line plugged.

A line that is open is not necessarily healthy. After clearing, we suggest a camera look through sewer inspection whenever the blockage was roots, a recurring problem or a house you have just bought.

Deciding between a one-off cleaning and a schedule

A main that plugs once after a flushed towel needs one cleaning and a change in habits. A main that plugs every spring is a maintenance question: how often to clear it, and when to stop clearing it and look at the pipe.

We ask when the last two blockages happened and what came back on the cable, then suggest an interval that matches. Two cleanings in a year on the same line is the usual point to add a camera look, because a third cabling rarely teaches you anything new.

When the backup is not a clog

On a septic system, sewage can rise because the tank is full or the field is saturated, and no cable will fix that. We clear the pipe between the house and the tank. We do not pump septic tanks or repair them, and a septic service has to handle that part.

Tell us which kind of system you have when you call, so we know whether to bring a machine or point you elsewhere.
