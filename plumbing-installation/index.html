Plumbing Installation in Etna, OH

If you smell gas, leave first

Leave the building immediately and do not use switches, phones or lighters inside. From outside, call your gas utility or 911 before you call any plumber. We never promise a response time for a gas smell, because the utility has the equipment and the authority to shut off and test the line. Our role comes afterward, with inspection, piping and appliance connections.

What an installation job actually includes

Plumbing installation covers new sinks, toilets, tubs and showers, new supply and drain lines, appliance connections and the rough-in for a building that does not have plumbing yet. It begins with a plan: where the fixtures sit, how the lines run and where they tie into existing pipes.

A good plan keeps drain slopes workable, vents sized, shutoffs reachable and walls open for as short a time as possible. Work is done to the Ohio Plumbing Code, and some jobs need a permit, which we will tell you about. Permit and inspection rules vary by jurisdiction, so ask the township or county building office.

The order a plumbing install follows

Planned work moves in stages, and the sequence protects both the schedule and the walls.

  1. 1

    Walk the site

    We look at the fixtures, the existing lines, the access and the water and drain tie-in points.

  2. 2

    Rough-in

    Drain, vent and supply lines are run inside the open walls or floor. In new construction this happens before drywall.

  3. 3

    Pressure and inspection

    The system is tested with air or water before it is covered. If a permit applies, the inspector sees it at this stage.

  4. 4

    Set fixtures

    Toilets, sinks, tubs and faucets go in once finishes are done. Supply lines and shutoffs are connected.

  5. 5

    Final test

    We run every fixture, check for leaks at joints and trap seals, and walk you through the shutoffs.

Gas line work: inspect, pipe and connect

Gas piping needs materials rated for it, proper support, correct sizing for the load and a pressure test before any appliance is lit. Installing a new range, dryer or water heater often means adding a drop, a shutoff and a sediment trap.

Backflow testing: a planned item to ask about

A backflow preventer stops used water from flowing back into the public supply. Whether yours needs periodic testing depends on the device and the local rules.

  • ✓Ask your water utility whether your property needs a backflow device
  • ✓Ask who is allowed to test it and how often
  • ✓Keep the device accessible and clear of snow, mulch and ice
  • ✓Call us if the device leaks, since repair is a plumbing job
  • ✓Tell us before an irrigation system or a new building goes in, because a device may be required

Where installation overlaps other services

A new fixture often exposes an old problem. Tying into worn lines can show the need for repiping, and a new bath on a low floor may call for an ejector pump. We would rather tell you that before the walls are closed up.

Fixtures, shutoffs and the details that get skipped

Every fixture should have its own shutoff within reach, because a single valve for the whole bathroom turns a dripping faucet into a house-wide outage. Braided stainless supply lines last longer than thin plastic ones, and a drain trap must hold a water seal to keep sewer gas out of the room.

Dishwashers need either an air gap or a high loop in the drain hose so that dirty water cannot siphon back. Washing machine boxes should sit at a height that lets the standpipe rise above the trap. These are small items on a drawing and large ones when a kitchen floods in the first month.

Which Etna installs need a county permit

The county health department oversees plumbing here, and plumbers must be registered with it. A permit is required to install, extensively alter or repair plumbing, with one exception, and inspections happen at rough-in, covered work and final. We tell you what applies before the first pipe is cut.

Usually no county permit

  • Replacing a toilet, faucet or disposal with the same or a similar fixture in the same spot
  • Anything else, ask us first, since only a like-for-like fixture swap is excluded

Permit and inspections apply

  • A new bathroom or a basement bath
  • Moving a sink, tub or toilet to a new location
  • New drain, vent or supply lines
  • New construction rough-in, which also meets the Ohio Plumbing Code (OAC 4101:3)
