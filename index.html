Plumbing emergency in Etna right now? Do these first

Water damage grows by the minute, so the first five minutes matter more than the plumber's arrival. Our emergency plumber line is staffed around the clock, and these steps are safe to do while you dial.

None of it needs a tool beyond what is already in the house.

Close the main valve

Turn the main shutoff clockwise until it stops. It is usually where the line enters the basement or utility room, near the meter if you have one.

Kill the water heater

If a tank is leaking, switch off its breaker or set a gas unit to pilot, then close the cold valve on top.

Stop using drains

A backup in the lowest fixture usually means the house line is full. Hold the laundry, the dishwasher and every flush.

Keep power and water apart

Stay out of standing water near outlets, the panel or a sump pump cord, and tell us about it on the call.

What the Etna record tells us about local plumbing

Etna Township sits in the southwestern corner of Licking County and had about 18,900 residents at the 2020 Census. Census estimates show most of its homes went up between 1990 and 2009, about 4,000 of roughly 7,100 occupied homes, with another 1,200 or so built in the 1970s. Only around 290 predate 1940, mostly near the village.

That mix shapes the calls. Newer homes mean PVC drains and PEX or copper supply, so the usual problems are failed fixtures, water heaters near the end of their life and grease clogs. The 1970s group is where galvanized or early copper still shows up. A short camera inspection sorts out which is which.

Rain, creeks and the weather record

The National Weather Service storm record for the area includes Purity Road closed by high water in June 2015 and Sycamore Creek out of its banks near Etna in July 2017. It also lists Watkins Road SW closed by high water in February 2022 and high water over York Road in June 2025.

For a basement, the practical step is simple. Test the pit on a dry day by pouring in a bucket of water and watching the float lift. If you want to dig into the causes, the sump pump and basement waterproofing pages explain what is plumbing and what is drainage.

Water, sewer and who to call first

Public water and sewer in much of the township come from the Licking Regional Water District, which draws groundwater from wells and treats it with filtration that removes calcium and magnesium. The district says its inspectors are on call around the clock for emergencies. The district publishes no hardness figure, so test your own tap before you buy any treatment equipment.

A leak between the street and your meter is the district's problem, and a leak after the meter is yours. If you are unsure where the line falls, call the district first for the street side and call us for the house side. The service line page explains how to tell the difference.

Permits and registration in Licking County

Plumbers who work in Licking County must be registered with the Licking County Health Department, and a permit is required for new plumbing and for installing, extensively altering or repairing it. The county rule excepts replacing a fixture with the same or similar fixture in the same location, with rough-in and final inspections on permitted work.

Rules differ by job, and the water heater question is one we answer address by address. Before work starts, we tell you what applies, and a homeowner can also check a plumber's registration with the health department's environmental office. The state licenses commercial contractors separately.
